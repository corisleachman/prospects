The core principle
External event = why now
Capability gap = why you

Best case: one verified event plus a second supporting signal.

The complete method

What the signal system is set up to do

Scan widely. Interrupt selectively.

01 · Monitor

Watch a changing prospect universe

Track founder-led independent creative, digital and social agencies, generally 10–100 people and especially 10–30. Follow the company plus founders, owners, MDs and relevant growth leaders.

Hundreds monitored
02 · Detect

Find evidence of change or pressure

Collect public events, posts, comments, hiring, leadership moves, offer changes and agency news. A source item is evidence. It only becomes a signal after its possible commercial meaning has been assessed.

Thousands scanned
03 · Judge

Separate useful evidence from noise

Classify, deduplicate and score every candidate signal. Prefer multiple related signals over one isolated event. Preserve the source URL, date and exact supporting evidence.

Only strong items surface
04 · Act

Recommend the smallest credible action

Decide whether to comment publicly, connect, send a private note, research further or wait. AI prepares the brief and draft. Coris reviews every message before anything is sent.

Human approval required
Signal library

Positive and negative signals

A positive event can create as much need as a problem.

Momentum and investment

Positive signals

Growth ambitionExpansion into a new market, an aggressive growth target, office opening, acquisition or public statement of ambition.Response: recognise the direction, then ask what must change commercially to support it.
Team expansionFirst commercial hire, senior marketing or growth appointment, several delivery hires, or rapid headcount growth.Response: explore whether the operating model, ownership and pipeline are ready for the extra capacity.
Offer developmentNew service, capability, sector proposition, productised offer, AI offer or partnership.Response: ask how the offer is being packaged, evidenced and taken to market.
Positioning changeRebrand, new website, proposition rewrite, clearer specialism or new market narrative.Response: offer an informed outside view. Never turn a positive launch into unsolicited criticism.
Commercial momentumMajor client win, strong run of wins, award, funding, senior promotion or visible business confidence.Response: focus on maintaining momentum and converting success into repeatable growth.
Founder perspectiveA founder asks a relevant question, describes a priority, shares a lesson or discusses growth systems and AI.Response: engage with the stated view first. A private message is justified only when you can add something useful.
Pressure and constraint

Negative signals

System pressureFounder wearing too many hats, bursts of marketing, disconnected tools, weak CRM adoption, slow follow-up or nobody clearly owning growth.Response: frame the gap as a question and offer a practical diagnostic or example workflow.
Pipeline qualityReferral dependence, unpredictable opportunities, low conversion, poor forecasting or a pipeline that cannot be trusted.Response: discuss visibility, qualification and consistency. Don't imply guaranteed leads.
Commercial pressureClient loss, concentration risk, redundancies, contraction, profit pressure, stalled growth or a missed target.Response: handle carefully. Research before contacting and never exploit bad news in the opening.
Hiring frictionRepeated vacancy, long-open role, difficulty finding senior growth talent or a role that combines too many responsibilities.Response: ask whether the need is really a hire, a system gap, or both.
Positioning confusionBroad capability list, inconsistent story, unclear differentiation or several offers competing for attention.Response: treat this as a hypothesis, not a diagnosis. Use a specific observation and invite their view.
Explicit frustrationA founder openly describes inconsistent new business, lack of time, poor technology adoption, content pressure or uncertainty about AI.Response: mirror their language lightly, acknowledge the issue and offer one relevant idea or question.
Qualification

Score before contacting

Research or outreach starts at 7/10.

Signal threshold
7/10

The score is a decision aid, not permission to invent a problem. A high score still needs verified evidence and a credible, low-pressure reason to contact the person.

DimensionScoreQuestion
Trigger0–3Is there a recent, specific external event or statement?
Need0–3Does the evidence suggest a commercially meaningful problem, pressure or opportunity?
Readiness0–2Is the agency visibly investing, changing or open to action now?
Fit0–2Does the agency and issue fit Coris's experience and offer?
0–3

Ignore or monitor

There is only ICP fit, generic activity or weak evidence. Store it without creating outreach.

4–6

Research further

There may be something useful, but the commercial implication or timing is not strong enough yet.

7–8

Prepare engagement

Choose a public comment, connection request or short private message based on the source and relationship.

9–10

Priority outreach

Multiple verified signals create a timely reason to act. Prepare a highly personalised note and clear value exchange.

Evidence standard

What counts as a real signal?

Never turn inference into fact.

01
RecentRecord when the event happened, not just when an article or repost was found.
02
SourceableKeep the direct URL, source type, publication date and a short exact extract or faithful note.
03
Commercially relevantExplain why it may affect growth, positioning, pipeline, capacity, systems or ownership.
04
Explicitly qualifiedSeparate the observed fact from your working hypothesis. Phrase the implication as a question.
05
DeduplicatedSeveral articles repeating one announcement are one event. Several different events may form a strong signal cluster.
06
Person-specificUse a founder's post or comment for their perspective. Don't transfer another employee's view to them.
Weak on their own

These are prompts to investigate, not automatic outreach reasons

  • An ordinary award announcement
  • A standard vacancy with no wider context
  • A new case study
  • A website refresh
  • General headcount growth
  • Infrequent posting
  • A generic AI post
  • A vague compliment about the work
Response method

Match the action to the evidence

Personalisation should be proportional to what you know.

Public perspectiveComment first. Respond to the idea in the post without steering immediately towards your service.
Strong business eventSend a brief private note connecting the event to one useful question or observation.
Possible but incomplete signalResearch or monitor. Don't manufacture a personalised opening from thin evidence.
Sensitive negative eventPause and investigate. Don't mention redundancies, losses or financial pressure opportunistically.
Minimal

One relevant reference

Use for an initial approach where information is limited. Be straightforward about relevance rather than pretending to know their problem.

Moderate

Signal plus working hypothesis

Use a verified event and one plausible commercial implication. Ask whether your reading is fair.

High

Signal cluster plus useful idea

Reserve for strategic prospects. Reference several connected facts and offer a tailored observation, workflow or relevant example.

Follow-up

Add value or wait

Check the last promise, stated decision date and relationship warmth. Follow up when there is new information or a useful next step, not just because time passed.

From signal to conversation

Don't confuse relevance with permission

The lightest credible action wins.

The rule

A signal is a reason to pay attention. It isn't permission to pitch.

The system should recommend the smallest useful action. Finding a relevant post may justify a comment, a connection or simply watching. It does not automatically justify a private sales message.

01 · Notice

Save and monitor

The signal is relevant but weak, ambiguous or sensitive. Keep it without creating outreach.

02 · Participate

Comment usefully

Respond to their stated idea in public. Add something to the discussion without steering towards your service.

03 · Connect

Stay in their world

Reference the real context briefly. Don't diagnose a problem or ask for a meeting in the connection request.

04 · Converse

Ask one question

Move privately when they engage, reply or the signal is strong enough to support a genuine question.

05 · Help

Offer useful value

Share an observation, checklist or example once you understand enough for it to be relevant.

06 · Explore

Suggest a conversation

Only suggest comparing notes or speaking after they acknowledge a priority, need or useful area to explore.

Pre-send check

Would this feel like a pitch-slap?

If it fails, reduce the action.

01

Does the message spend more time on them than it does on you?

02

Have you turned a possibility into an unsupported diagnosis?

03

Would the opening still make sense if you removed your service introduction?

04

Are you asking for more commitment than the evidence justifies?

05

Could essentially the same message have been sent to 100 other agency founders?

06

Have you earned the move from public engagement to private outreach?

Progression rule

Move when the context changes, not when a timer expires

Don't use a compulsory 48-hour delay. Progress when the person engages, a stronger signal appears or you have something genuinely useful to add. Otherwise, wait.

Prospect coverage

Use a portfolio, not a permanently fixed list

Maintain a priority group for active relationships and high-fit agencies, a rotating discovery group for new agencies, and a watchlist for weak or developing signals. Every completed profile check must be written back to the CRM, including checks that find no useful posts. The normal batch selector excludes anyone already checked; a rescan must be a deliberate choice. People found through keyword and conversation searches sit in a separate review queue and are checked against LinkedIn URL before being added to the CRM.
